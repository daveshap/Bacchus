# Hand-drawn Explainer

Use warm paper, dark pencil-like outlines, terracotta for emphasis, and muted greens for secondary elements. Give illustrated objects a coherent outline weight and simple silhouettes. Use subtle grain where clean outlines and readable text still come through.

Arrange each scene as a clear annotated sketch with one main idea. Use arrows, brackets, and cards to show relationships. Place captions in stable blank areas. Draw and frame figures so they read clearly at a small size. Keep numbers and important labels in regular, legible type.

Build the explanation in short beats. Reveal a label, object, or next step at its spoken cue. Use gentle fades, nearby movements, and short purposeful pushes. Let each diagram settle before the narration moves into a new idea. Keep repeated objects and their orientation consistent across scenes.

Use an Ambient bed under the narration with a warm, restrained tone. Reduce it over important phrases and settle with the final idea. Sound effects are off by default. When requested, use a soft page or reveal accent at selected cues.

Check that fine marks, captions, and silhouettes still read at viewing size. Review adjacent frames around timed changes and listen to the prepared mix over the densest explanation.
