## Production invariants and Bacchus coverage

**Preserve the supplied recording and bind timing to it.** The shared studio retains the exact source identity, duration and existing acceptance evidence. It can consume supplied word timing tied to the recording hash or derive it separately. The accepted spoken wording includes improvisation and repeated words must resolve to the intended occurrence. Bacchus now retains the original uploaded bytes and imports the existing word evidence by its recording hash. It groups verified word spans into editable scene cues. Its built-in transcription still estimates timing, and resolving ambiguous words or correcting cue positions needs a dedicated review tool. [Shared timing contract](C:/AnimationAutomationStudio/docs/timing.md:3) · [YouTube accepted transcript](C:/YouTube_Animations/recordings/PLAYBOOK.md:11)

**Plan the whole film and resolve artwork as one inventory.** The legacy plan specifies scene order, recorded cues, exact labels and data, actions, transitions, framing and artwork capabilities. The consolidated request includes the scene use, identity, poses, parts, clipping/overlap and transparency. Bacchus has whole-script scene coverage, known layouts and motions, one request for artwork IDs and an artwork gate before preview. Its current spec is much smaller. Project-local uploaded PNGs represent a useful first asset path, while shared-library lookup, approved-source lineage and checks for display size, parts, poses and suitability still need an adapter. The legacy G1 exit includes verified access to suitable source material. Bacchus can show a reviewed draft earlier, with a distinct artwork-blocked status. [Planning and consolidated request](C:/AnimationAutomationStudio/docs/playbooks/01-planning.md:15) · [Asset preparation and proofs](C:/AnimationAutomationStudio/asset-playbook/PLAYBOOK.md:25) · [Shared catalog](C:/AnimationAutomationStudio/ASSETS.md:3)

**Use a style to encode capabilities as well as appearance.** In the existing work, warm editorial includes approved illustration and editable teaching graphics, YouTube uses its hand-drawn figures and diagrams, and BFG has reusable equipment and operational UI with precise cue-driven states. Bacchus currently uses three distinct visual treatments over shared title, editorial and illustration layouts plus simple whole-scene motion. These treatments establish the rendering path. Native chart, diagram, multi-layer and technical component capabilities still need to be modeled as typed scene data and implemented once as trusted renderer actions. [Warm editorial](C:/AnimationAutomationStudio/docs/styles/warm-editorial.md:3) · [YouTube style](C:/AnimationAutomationStudio/docs/styles/youtube.md:3) · [BFG motion and semantic color](C:/BFG_Animations/docs/styles/bfg-technical.md:38)

**Finish the soundtrack before QC and review that exact mix.** The current legacy protocol expects music unless explicitly declined, with optional effects and reels scoped separately. It keeps the narration timeline and the entire recording, derives a distinct mix, and records both identities. Bacchus provides explicit music selection or opt-out, timed imported effects, narration-first ducking and a review WAV from the same mix graph as MP4. Its stereo 48 kHz output and 720p24 review renderer are new choices; the legacy studio uses generated mono 44.1 kHz audio and generally delivers 4K60. Composing a film-specific score from story beats, collecting source-relative intelligibility/loudness evidence, and profile-specific delivery settings remain gaps. [Legacy audio boundary and format](C:/AnimationAutomationStudio/docs/audio-handoff.md:7) · [Animation handoff soundtrack](C:/AnimationAutomationStudio/docs/playbooks/03-animation.md:18)

**Freeze the candidate, inspect motion, then verify the encoded result.** A real shared-studio candidate recorded 72 hashed dependencies, a selected soundtrack and 4K60 settings, with a separate evidence-bearing acceptance record. The stage checklist asks for scene entrances, relevant cues, busiest frames, exits and adjacent boundary frames, then samples the actual encoded file. BFG's production record shows visual review finding opacity grouping and transition defects after automated checks. Bacchus already has explicit user approval, candidate invalidation, renderer preview and stream verification. Evidence-backed timed visual QC and encoded-frame comparisons are the next substantive difference. The prior method assigned acceptance to the production lead unless the user had placed a hold. Bacchus's explicit user approvals are a product choice. ## Highest-value gaps

1. Add automatic measured timing and a cue review tool for recordings supplied without word evidence. Include first and last spoken content and repeated-word cases in fixtures.
2. Connect one real, read-only library/profile to the planner and renderer. Start with a small allowlist of canonical warm artwork and a few reusable approved actions. Surface unsupported uses and sizing/part requirements in the same consolidated request.
3. Build one review surface with evidence for scene holds, timed actions, transitions and audio intelligibility. Bind review observations and sampled encoded frames to the candidate/media identity. Use the real scoring-revision and transition defects as regression cases.
4. Validate a production-length pilot before adding optional deliveries. The existing outtakes, forest-walk editing, voice generation and downstream publication each have distinct handoffs. Bring each in only when its workflow is part of the requested Bacchus scope.
